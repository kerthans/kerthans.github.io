## Background

In managed IT services, most deals eventually end up in a proposal. It is not just sales material. It is where scope, pricing, recurring revenue, and trust first sit in one place.

I wanted to treat quoting as a system, not another nicely formatted document.

## The idea

Build a tool around the full quote-to-signature flow: quote editing, MRR calculation, one-time projects, templates, proposal layout, online acceptance, signature capture, win data, and PDF export.

The core assumption was simple: if the quote and proposal share the same commercial structure, the path from service work to signature gets shorter.

## What I built

The system has three layers. The construction layer is a quote editor that understands recurring and one-time work, with templates carrying pricing logic instead of just styling. The presentation layer renders the same structure into a client-facing proposal. The signing layer lets the proposal move directly to a decision, with PDF export as a fallback.

The important decision was shared structure: the editor and the proposal page render the same business model.

## Where it is now

ProQuote is live. It can create quotes, generate proposals, collect signatures, and export PDFs.

After the product worked, the bottleneck moved from “can this be built?” to “how does this enter a customer buying workflow?”

## What happened next

After shipping, implementation stopped being the main uncertainty. The real question became reach: who needs it first, what workflow it belongs in, and whether the proposal is the entry point or the final product.

## Next

Keep it running. Do not add a larger feature layer until the distribution path is clearer.

## Still open

Some uncertainty cannot be solved with better architecture. The product can quote, propose, and collect signatures. The next test is whether those capabilities line up with real distribution needs.
